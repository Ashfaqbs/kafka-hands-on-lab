STEP 3 (a) --Replication Factor : 
.\bin\windows\kafka-topics.bat  --create --bootstrap-server localhost:9092 --replication-factor 1 --partitions 9 --topic mytutTopic

In summary, --replication-factor 1 is used for creating a topic with no replicas, which is suitable for non-critical environments or for testing purposes. For production environments, it's recommended to use a replication factor of at least 2 or 3 to ensure data availability and fault tolerance .

https://www.cloudkarafka.com/blog/apache-kafka-replication-factor-perfect-number.html
https://www.javatpoint.com/kafka-topic-replication


When you set up a Kafka topic like in your example with --replication-factor 1 and --partitions 9, you end up with 9 partitions, each with only one copy (one replica). This means there’s no redundancy—if the broker hosting any of these partitions fails, the data within that partition becomes unavailable until the broker is restored or another solution is implemented.

If you change the setup to use --replication-factor 2, each of the 9 partitions will then have one additional copy, making for a total of two replicas per partition. These replicas will be distributed across different brokers in your Kafka cluster (assuming your cluster has at least two brokers). Here's how it enhances the system:

Fault Tolerance: With two replicas, if a broker fails, there is still another broker that has a copy of the same partition data. Kafka can switch to this copy to continue serving data without interruption.
Data Safety: Having an additional copy means there's less risk of data loss. Even if one copy is corrupted or lost, the other can be used as a backup.
Load Management: Reads can potentially be load-balanced between the two replicas, depending on the consumer configuration and Kafka version specifics, although writes will always go through the leader replica.
Just remember that to truly benefit from replication, your Kafka cluster must have enough brokers to distribute replicas across. If you have a replication factor of 2 but only one broker, Kafka won't be able to create the second replica, and thus, won't fully achieve the desired fault tolerance.

Change the Kafka and Zookeeper data and log directories: By default, Kafka and ZooKeeper store their data and logs in temporary directories. If the paths to these directories are too long, you can change them to directories with shorter paths. This can be done by editing the config/server.properties and config/zookeeper.properties files [11][12][14].



# Change Kafka log dirs in server.properties
log.dirs=D:/kafka/data/kafka-logs

# Change Zookeeper data dir in zookeeper.properties
dataDir=D:/kafka/data/zookeeper
